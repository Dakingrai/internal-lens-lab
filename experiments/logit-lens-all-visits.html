Where the logit lens finds the intermediate: all 48 layer visits on the practice questions

Done

In short

The starter entry reads the logit lens at 10 layer visits that its authors chose and passes 6 of the 10 practice questions. This run reads the same lens at all 48 visits, to see at which of them the intermediate is among the top tokens, and what the lens says in its place where it is not.

It is a first look, not a test. It was run before this page was written, so nothing below was predicted in advance, and the sections that would hold predictions say so.

What we do

The model is Inkling 2 Mini, run on NDIF. Each of the 10 public practice questions is put to it as the challenge does: the exact model input, one forward pass, read at the input's last token. At each of the 48 layer visits we take the state entering the visit and apply the model's own final norm and unembedding to it. That is the starter's logit lens, unchanged, with no trained weights.

Two things are kept for each question and visit:

  • the lens's 10 highest-scoring tokens, best first, which is what an entry would hand to the judge;
  • the rank of the intermediate in the whole vocabulary: the rank of the best token that writes the intermediate exactly, in any case, with or without a leading space.

Exact matching is stricter than the leaderboard's judge, which also accepts inflections and translations, so a rank here can understate what the judge would credit.

The three runs of layers 6 to 17 do not follow on from one another. In the model's code each run starts again from the state that left layer 5, and the second and third also receive messages from the run before. The row for layer 6 is therefore the same in all three runs.

Second part: the WorkspaceBench single-token questions

Added on 24 Nov 2026, after the practice run. The same lens, read the same way, on the 238 single-token questions of WorkspaceBench as adapted to this model (the project's Dataset page): basic readout 53, multihop 97, multilingual 88.

  • The basic questions are put to the model as the challenge puts the practice ones: in its chat format with thinking off, read at the last token.
  • The multihop and multilingual questions are fed as plain text, as the bank says they are meant to be. Multihop is read at the prompt's last word, multilingual at its last token.
  • A token counts as the intermediate when it is the bank's word for it or one of the other forms the bank lists, exactly, in any case. A question is found when that holds for one of the top 10 tokens at one or more of the 48 visits. With 48 chances this is a generous measure, so the count per visit is reported beside it.

Competing explanations

None were set out before the run. Where the lens fails, the readouts alone cannot separate two readings: the model has not formed the intermediate at that visit, or it has and the final unembedding cannot read it there. Telling these apart needs a second lens or an intervention on the same visits.

What we expect

Nothing was written down before the practice run. The one prior was Goodfire's report that the logit lens works on this model only at the end of each loop.

Second part

Written on 24 Nov 2026 by the agent running it, after the practice run and before the second part ran. The project's owner has not reviewed these.

  • Basic readout. The practice split repeats. Of the 27 questions about an athlete, a landmark or a musician, the intermediate is found on 22 or more. Of the 25 about arithmetic, a day or a month, it is found on 5 or fewer.
  • Which visits. The visits that found anything on the practice questions (24, 33 to 36, 43 to 47) account for 90% or more of every question-and-visit pair where the intermediate is in the top 10.
  • Multihop. The intermediate is the bridge, which the model never writes, so the lens finds it less often than a basic fact: on fewer than half of the 97. Where it is found, it is more often at visits 24 or 33 to 36 than at 43 to 47, where the lens should be reading the answer.
  • Multilingual. The English concept is found on more than half of the 88, mostly at visits 24 and 33 to 36, with the late visits reading the word in the prompt's language.

If the basic split does not repeat, the practice result was an accident of ten questions. If the multihop bridge is found as often as a basic fact, the lens reads more than the next token.

What would make us distrust a result

Written after the run, as the limits of what it shows:

  • 10 questions, one or two of each kind, one forward pass each. Any count below is out of 10, and a kind's behaviour rests on one or two prompts.
  • The layer 6 rows being identical across the three runs looked like a mistake in the read. It was checked against the model's code and is how the loop is built.
  • The ranks use exact matching, not the judge.
  • Nobody but the agent that ran it has read these readouts yet.

Against what we expected:

  • Basic readout: as expected. The intermediate is found on 22 of the 27 questions about an athlete, a landmark or a musician (22 of the 23 that can be scored), and on 4 of the 25 about arithmetic, a day or a month. By kind: athlete 9 of 9 scoreable, landmark 8 of 8, musician 5 of 6, arithmetic 3 of 6, day before 1 of 6, day after 0 of 5, month after 0 of 8. The practice split was not an accident of ten questions.
  • Which visits: as expected for two files, just short for the third. The practice run's ten visits hold 96% of the question-and-visit pairs in the top 10 for basic readout, 95% for multihop and 89% for multilingual, against a threshold of 90%. In all three, visits 43 to 46 hold most of them and visit 36 is the best of the looped ones. On multilingual, visits 40 and 42 also find some.
  • Multihop: not as expected. We expected the bridge to be found on fewer than half; it is found on 60 of the 79 that can be scored. We expected it at visits 24 and 33 to 36 more than at 43 to 47; it is found at the late visits on 56 questions and at the middle ones on 21. One reading is that the lens sees more than the next token. Another, which this run cannot rule out, is that on a plain-text prompt the bridge is itself a likely next word, as iron is after "the element with atomic number 26 is".
  • Multilingual: found as expected, but not where. The English concept is found on 87 of the 88. We expected it mostly at the middle visits with the late ones reading the prompt's language; instead it is found at visits 43 to 47 on 85 questions and at 24 or 33 to 36 on 40.

The basic file is the one put to the model as the challenge does. On it the lens's limit is the same as on the practice questions: it reads a fact the model is about to state, and almost never the result of a step in a sequence.
